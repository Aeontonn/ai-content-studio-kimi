# Copywriting: hooks, captions, CTAs, Stories, email

Patterns, not scripts. Adapt every one to the brand voice in the profile, and to the
audience's own words.

## 1. Hook types (vary them; max two of a type per week)

| Type | Pattern | Example shape |
|---|---|---|
| Contrarian | "Stop X. Do Y." / "X is overrated" | "Stop journaling every morning." |
| Mistake | "The mistake that keeps you..." | "The one mistake making your routine fail" |
| Specific result | number + outcome + timeframe | "What 10 minutes a day did in 30 days" |
| Question | a question they can't ignore | "Why do you quit every routine by Thursday?" |
| Curiosity gap | open a loop, close it later | "Nobody talks about the night before." |
| List | number + promise | "3 reasons your routine keeps failing" |
| Identity | "If you're a ___ who ___" | "If you're not a morning person, watch this" |
| Before / after | show the change | "My mornings in January vs now" |
| Story opener | in medias res | "I overslept on day 3 and almost quit." |
| How-to | "How to X without Y" | "How to build a routine without waking up earlier" |
| Myth | "You've been told X. It's wrong." | "You don't need a 5 a.m. alarm." |
| POV / relatable | "POV: ..." | "POV: your 90-minute routine on a Monday" |

Rules: specific beats clever; under ~10 words spoken; the visual hook (movement,
an object, text) starts at frame 1; the hook's promise must be paid off in the piece.

## 2. Captions

The first line is a second hook (it's all people see before "more"). Then:
- **Short:** hook line, then 1 line of value, then the CTA.
- **Value list:** hook line, then 3-5 short lines or numbered points, then the CTA.
- **Story:** hook line, then the story in 3-6 short paragraphs, then the lesson, then the CTA.

Short paragraphs, line breaks, at most 1-3 emoji if the voice allows. Put the main keyword in
the first sentence (Instagram search reads captions). 3-5 specific hashtags at the end
(or none, if the brand prefers).

## 3. CTAs by goal

| Goal | CTAs |
|---|---|
| Reach | "Send this to someone who...", "Share this with your..." |
| Saves | "Save this for tomorrow morning", "Save it before you need it" |
| Comments | "Comment WORD and I'll send you...", "Which one are you: A or B?" |
| Follows | "Follow for one calm habit a week", the series promise |
| Clicks / sales | "Link in bio for...", "Grab it before Sunday" (true deadlines only) |
| Email list | "The full version is in Thursday's newsletter, link in bio" |
| Replies (Stories, email) | "Reply with...", "Tap the slider if..." |

One primary CTA per piece. Match the week's goal.

## 4. Story sequences (one item = 3-7 frames)

Patterns:
- **Poll opener:** question with a poll, then the answer and why, then a tip, then a link or reply CTA.
- **Behind the scenes:** what I'm doing today, a detail, the lesson, a question box.
- **Promo runway:** problem, then the offer, then proof (a DM screenshot with consent), then the link sticker plus deadline.
- **Repost plus context:** share the new Reel or carousel, then a sticker "did you see this?", then the main tip.

One interactive sticker per set; text large and short; first frame must make them tap forward.

## 5. Email newsletters

- **Subject:** 30-50 characters, curiosity or a specific benefit, no clickbait. Write 2 options.
- **Preview text:** 40-90 characters that extend the subject, never repeat it.
- **Body:** personal opening (a moment or story), the idea in more depth than social,
  1 practical step, one CTA (button or link), a P.S. with a secondary angle or soft CTA.
- 150-400 words for a weekly letter. Write like one person to one person.

## 6. On-screen text and keywords

- Reel hook text: 3-7 words, high contrast, upper third, 2-3 s.
- Use searchable keywords (what people type into Instagram or TikTok search) in hook text,
  caption opening and Shorts titles.
- Numbers as digits ("3 reasons"), not words.

## 7. Voice check (run on every piece before presenting)

1. Would the creator actually say this out loud? (Read it as speech.)
2. Does it use the audience's words from the profile, not marketing words?
3. Does it follow the profile's do/don't list and avoid its banned words?
4. Is there exactly one idea and one CTA?
5. Is anything claimed that the creator can't back up? Remove it.

## 8. Writing in Swedish (if the profile says so)

- Write natively, don't translate English hooks word for word. "Spara den här till imorgon"
  is right; English idioms often feel off.
- Keep anglicisms only if the brand voice uses them (many Swedish creators mix in English
  words: follow the profile).
- Use "du" (singular, informal) unless the profile says otherwise.
- Swedish captions run longer; keep lines short and put the keyword first.
- Common fillers to cut in video: "eh", "öh", "liksom", "typ", "alltså", "asså".


## 9. Writing in Chinese (中文文案)

Use when the profile or the user asks for Chinese. Write natively; never translate an English
hook word for word.

- **Hooks 开头:** 15 characters or fewer on screen, the conflict or benefit first. Patterns that
  work: 反常识 ("别再早起了"), 数字 ("3 个原因"), 提问 ("为什么你的习惯总坚持不到周四？"),
  身份 ("如果你不是早起型的人"), 前后对比 ("一月 vs 现在").
- **Captions 文案:** first line repeats or sharpens the hook. Short lines, one idea per line,
  numbered points with 1. 2. 3. Use full-width punctuation (，。！？：) in Chinese text, and a
  space between Chinese and English words or numbers is optional; follow the profile.
- **Tone:** prefer "你" (informal) unless the profile says "您". Avoid empty phrases such as
  "干货满满", "建议收藏", "绝绝子" unless the brand voice uses them deliberately.
- **CTA:** "收藏起来明天早上用", "转发给需要的朋友", "评论区告诉我你选哪个", "点主页链接领取".
- **Hashtags:** for Instagram, mix 2-3 Chinese and 1-2 English tags only if the audience searches
  both; keep 3-5 in total.
- **Subtitles 字幕:** about 16 characters per line, at most 2 lines; drop 嗯/呃 from the text.
- **Mixed-language brands:** the profile decides caption language vs on-screen language; when
  unclear, ask once.
